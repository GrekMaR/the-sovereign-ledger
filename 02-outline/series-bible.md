# The Sovereign Ledger — Series Bible (Book One, Chapters 1–10)

This is the single source of truth for drafting. If a chapter needs a fact that is not here, keep it small, unnamed, and non-load-bearing. Do not invent new named gods, houses, currencies, or tiers.

---

## 1. Premise in one line

Theo Marsh, a London turnaround consultant who specialised in distressed debt, dies and wakes in the body of **Lysander "Lys" Vey**, 24, the drunk, disgraced heir of a bankrupt guild in a city where the gods auction everything — rain, roads, dungeons, and the right to exist as a guild. He has ten days before the guild is foreclosed and the bond on its Charter Stone kills him.

## 2. The world

### The city
- **Vessant** — a river-delta trade city climbing the flank of a mountain whose summit touches the Firmament. Lower city by the river (warehouses, tanneries, docks), middle city (guildhalls, the Corn Hall), upper city (noble houses, temples).
- **The Saltreach** — the broad, slow river through Vessant; the only bulk route for grain from outside the delta. About **84 grain barges** work it.
- **The Harrowdales** — southern valleys two days' ride away; the breadbasket that grows roughly **40% of Vessant's grain**.
- **Tallow Street** — lower city; the Cinderwick guildhall (smells of old tallow; the guild began as chandlers and carters).
- **Mourngate Granary** — a huge stone granary on the Saltreach bank held by Cinderwick on a 99-year lease with 11 years left. Holds **18,000 sacks of old barley** Harlan Vey bought at the top of the market.
- **The Corn Hall** — the grain exchange in the middle city; prices chalked on slate boards.
- **Scale Street / the Temple of the Balance** — lower temple of Sael, where Brother Oren serves.
- **Low Wharrow** — a derelict riverside warehouse district with failing flood-levees.
- **The Cindermaw** — a dungeon in the mountain's root; source of **emberstone** (fuel for forges, lamps, and ward-lanterns). Its entrance passage is **the Deepway**.
- **The Dawn Stair** — a stair of white stone that appears at first light on auction days, climbing from the Temple of the Balance up into the sky to **the Exchange** (the Firmament Exchange): a marble floor above the clouds, no walls, a gallery of forty carved Seats.

### The gods (the Scaled Pantheon)
Gods appear at the Exchange as tall masked figures; they never show faces. The auctioneer's hammer is Sael's.
- **Sael the Weigher** — commerce, measure, the auction itself. Presides with the Hammer. Voice like a bell under water.
- **Ilvenne the Harvest-Mother** — rain, grain, fields.
- **Korrath the Oathkeeper** — debts, vows, contracts. Runs the **Vault of Vows** (divine lending) through priestesses.
- **Maruth Below** — dungeons, mines, deep things. Sells his lots only at the Deep Hammer at the Cindermaw mouth.
- **Oda of the Roads** — roads and rivers (mention only; not a lot-seller in Book One except in background).

Why gods auction: they feed on **grace** — faith and kept promises made measurable. They sell rights to whoever will generate and pay the most grace over time. Most mortals assume the gods want the biggest lump sum. They don't. (Lys only fully understands this in Chapter 8.)

### Money and grace
- Coin: **crown** (gold) = 20 **marks** (silver); **bits** are copper small change.
- **Grace** is measured in **gleams**. Grace accrues to a guild's Charter Stone from tithes, prayer, and contracts fulfilled under divine witness. Temples will convert coin to grace at about **120 crowns per gleam** but never convert grace back to coin. Grace is scarce and sticky.
- Auction bids are made in grace. A bidder must post a **bid bond of 20 gleams** (returned if they lose).

### Seats, charters, and the Guild Court
- There are **40 Seats** at the Exchange; only a chartered guild holding a Seat may bid. Seats cannot be sold. If a guild is foreclosed, the **Guild Court** (mortal, noble-dominated) awards its Seat to the foreclosing creditor if that creditor is a chartered house. **That is why House Dain wants Cinderwick: for Seat 39.**
- **Divine stay:** a charter won at the Exchange puts the winning guild under the god's protection; no mortal writ may seize it for **90 days**.

### The Charter Stone and Standing (the "system")
Every chartered guild has a Charter Stone — a waist-high block of grey stone in its hall with a vein of light. It displays **Standing**, a number Sael computes from grace held, holdings, and kept contracts. Standing sets a guild's **Tier**, and Tier gates which lots it may bid on.

| Tier | Name | Standing | May bid on |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | Tin Seat | 0–999 | minor blessings, rights, tithes |
| II | Chartered House | 1,000–4,999 | + district charters |
| III | Warden House | 5,000–19,999 | + dungeon concessions |
| IV | Sovereign House | 20,000+ | + sovereign (land-with-lordship) charters |

Notification format (use sparingly — at most three per chapter, only on real change):

```text
⟦ CINDERWICK COMPANY — CHARTER STONE ⟧
STANDING: 212 (Tier I — Tin Seat)
SEAT 39 — UNDER LIEN
GRACE: 9 gleams
```

### The Guildmaster's Bind and Ledger-sight (Lys's personal power)
- The guildmaster is **bound** to the Charter Stone. If the guild is foreclosed or dissolved, the bound master suffers **Unbinding** — it usually kills. Harlan Vey's heir is bound now. This is personal stakes, not just money.
- The Bind grants **Ledger-sight**: Lys can see contracts made under divine witness as **threads of light** — gold for kept, grey for pending, red-black for broken or breaking.
- **Costs:** a nail-behind-the-eye headache, nosebleeds; holding it too long causes blackouts. It cannot be used casually.
- **Growth by Tier:**
  - Tier I: only contracts he is physically touching (Ch 1–3).
  - Tier II: every witnessed contract in the room he is in (from Ch 4).
  - Tier III: he can read **strain** — how close a contract is to breaking (default risk) (from Ch 7).
  - Tier IV: glimpses the city's web of obligations at once (Ch 10 only, briefly, and it nearly floors him).
- Progression beats must follow the style guide: an understanding or choice triggers the gain; the new capability is used in action; the gain has a consequence.

### Theo / Lys
- Theo Marsh, 38 in his old life: turnaround and distressed-debt consultant, London. Died suddenly (heart gave out on a late train — keep vague). Woke in Lys's body **four days before Chapter 1**.
- The original Lys drank himself into a stupor and did not wake. Theo has patchy access to Lys's memories — faces, streets, childhood — like a smudged ledger.
- **Nobody knows.** He never tells anyone in Book One. People notice he stopped drinking and started "talking like a counting-house". Veyra suspects something is off.
- He thinks in modern terms (cash runway, debt-for-equity, jump bids, chokepoints, prisoner's dilemma) and must translate them into the world's language. Show the translation — do not lecture the reader. Modern jargon appears in his head, rarely in dialogue.
- Flaw: treats people as line items when frightened; mistrusts sentiment. Arc: learns that trust and kept promises are the real asset in a world that literally runs on them.

## 6. Chapter plan (beats, state in, state out)

Every chapter: Lys POV, close third, past tense. 3,200–4,000 words. Open in motion. Close on a decision, cost, revelation, or unanswered question — never mere stoppage. At least one scene of real dialogue conflict. Economics must be concrete (numbers, goods, people), not abstract.

### Chapter 1 — The Bankrupt Guild (Seedmoon 3)
**State in:** Lys four days in the body, sober, terrified, reading the books at night. Guild: 14 members, 212 crowns, debts as above.
**Beats:**
1. Opening: Crell nails the **Tenth-Day Writ** to the guildhall door at dawn. Lys meets him on the step. Crell is polite and merciless: 41,000 crowns by Seedmoon 13 or the Guild Court forecloses; Seat 39 goes to House Dain. Lys touches the writ — first on-page **Ledger-sight** (grey-gold thread binding it to Korrath's witness; it's valid). Nosebleed.
2. Inside, the Charter Stone flickers its notification (Standing 212, Seat 39 under lien). Pell explains, frightened, what Unbinding does to a bound master. Lys realises the foreclosure kills him.
3. Mira Thorne arrives with three teamsters to collect four months' wages and quit. Conflict: she despises the old drunk Lys. He can't pay. He does something the old Lys never did: shows her the actual books and tells her the truth. Offers her scrip — a share in the guild's future instead of wages (debt-for-equity, translated as "a stake in the Company, witnessed at the temple"). She doesn't accept yet.
4. Walk to Mourngate with Mira and Hobb: 18,000 sacks of barley worth 3 marks each — 2,700 crowns, "a monument to your father's timing." Nobody wants barley.
5. Turning point: at the Temple of the Balance on Scale Street, Brother Oren posts the Lesser Hammer lot list for Seedmoon 9. Lot 7: **"The Rain-Tithe of the Harrowdales — forfeit by House Oskel — rebid. Rain suspended one season pending tithe."** Everyone reads it as a dull lot. Lys, reading it twice, realises: no blessed rain on the Harrowdales this growing season → the harvest fails → grain prices will climb for months. The market hasn't priced it. Oren confirms, drily, that a divine charter won at the Exchange stays mortal writs for 90 days.
6. Close (decision): Back at the hall, Lys lays it out to Mira: they will not sell the barley; they will bid for the Rain-Tithe — which needs a bid bond and far more grace than their 9 gleams. The only people who want rain on the Harrowdales more than anyone are the people who farm it. He asks Mira to ride south tonight and sell the farmers their own rain. She takes the scrip. Final line lands on the cost: if this fails, he doesn't lose a guild — he dies.

**State out:** Mira riding south. Oren an interested sceptic. Crell reported to Dain that the boy is sober.

### Chapter 2 — The First Charter (Seedmoon 8–9)
**State in:** Five days of scraping. Lys has settled the 14 members with scrip; wrote a pledge instrument with Oren.
**Beats:**
1. Opening: night of Seedmoon 8, Mira rides into Tallow Street half-dead with fatigue and a satchel of village pledge-tokens: **180 gleams from 31 villages.** The deal (a pre-sale): villages pledge grace now; if Cinderwick wins, they get rain at a fixed low rain-rent for ten years. What it cost her: she had to swear on her own name.
2. Oren walks the pledges through the temple, converting them to Charter Stone grace. Stone updates (grace 189).
3. Dawn: the **Dawn Stair** appears. First sight of the Exchange above the clouds; the masked gods; the forty Seats; the Concord houses in their silks. Aurel Dain greets Lys with genuine courtesy and a veiled threat.
4. Auction of Lot 7 (open ascending, grace; the Hammer falls after three heartbeats of silence). Lys has done the auction theory: most bidders value it low; one proxy — a Lantern Syndicate proxy, bidding for House Oskel, who wants her tithe back — values it higher. Lys uses a **jump bid** to signal deep pockets he doesn't have, then must decide whether to go to the edge of his 189. He wins at **164 gleams**. Tension through the bidding; Ledger-sight when he touches the charter scroll — his first gold thread with a god.
5. Charter Stone: Standing 588; "Divine charter held — writs stayed until Emberwane 9."
6. Close: Aurel Dain, pleasant, on the Stair: "You've bought rain for a desert, Master Vey. And you've told every house on that floor that your guild has a plan. Plans can be read." Lys realises he's now visible — and that Dain holds his debt note whether or not the writ is stayed. The 90-day clock starts. Cost/unanswered question close.

**State out:** Grace left: 25 gleams (189 − 164). Rain-Tithe held (rain resumes Mistmoon). Stay until Emberwane 9. Standing 588.

### Chapter 3 — The Price of Silence (Seedmoon 10–18)
**Beats:**
1. Opening: Pell wakes Lys — Mourngate was broken into overnight; **1,200 sacks** stolen, lamp oil poured on more. Hobb hurt (bruised, not dead).
2. Same week: a whisper campaign in the Corn Hall and the Harrowdale roads: Cinderwick's rain-shares are a fraud; the boy is drunk again; the pledges will never be honoured. Village elders send riders demanding their grace back — a bank run on trust.
3. Crell visits with the "price of silence": House Dain will buy the barley at 5 marks, cover the losses, and the rumours will stop — if Cinderwick sells the Rain-Tithe charter to Dain. Lys refuses. Crell: "Silence has a price, Master Vey. So does noise."
4. Lys chooses radical transparency (crisis communication translated): with Oren as witness, he opens the guild's books in the Temple forecourt, has Pell read the ledger aloud, shows the charter scroll, and lets anyone touch the Charter Stone's readout brought on a cart (or have Oren read it). Villagers' riders see it. It holds — mostly.
5. Mira tracks the stolen sacks to a Dain-leased barge. Prosecution in a Dain-dominated court is unwinnable. Lys negotiates a public settlement instead: the thieves' employer "unknown", but House Dain pays restitution at 5 marks per stolen sack "out of neighbourly concern" — humbling Dain without cornering him. Lys learns the difference between winning and making an enemy who must retaliate.
6. Close (revelation): a Lantern Syndicate courier — a child with a lantern-shaped pin — delivers an unsigned note: the Gilded Concord has sold **60,000 sacks** forward for Harvestmoon 1 delivery at 4 marks. They are short grain in a year without rain. And an invitation: "The Lantern Market opens on the next moonless night. Bring your appetite. — V."

**State out:** Standing ~610. Trust shaken but holding. Lys knows the Concord's short position.

### Chapter 4 — The Lantern Market (Seedmoon 29 – Greenmoon 4)
**Beats:**
1. Opening: moonless night; Lys and Mira descend under the Dawn Stair's foundations into the **Lantern Market** — hundreds of paper lanterns, whispered prices, brokers trading **shadow notes** (promises on future auction results and future goods — this world's futures and options).
2. Veyra receives him. Sharp, playful, dangerous conversation. She knows the Rain-Tithe logic. She asks what he'll do with it. He tests her; she tests him. She suspects he isn't who he was ("Lysander Vey couldn't count his fingers sober").
3. Insight: the harvest will fail, but the Concord will try to import grain from outside the delta by river to meet its forward contracts. The only bulk route is the Saltreach, by barge. Barges are idle and cheap because of the glut. **Control the middle of the chain.** Lys proposes leasing **60 of the 84** barges for six months at 2 crowns/week each — but he has no coin.
4. Deal with Veyra: the Syndicate fronts the lease money through shadow notes; in exchange the Syndicate takes **20% of the barge-lease profits** and a favour to be named later. Lys accepts, eyes open.
5. Over the next days: the network — Mira's teamsters, Hobb's warehousemen, river shrines where barge-masters pray (Oda's roadside shrines) — Lys signs barge-masters one by one; barley price creeps to 4 marks; first dry week in the Harrowdales.
6. Progression: when the 60th lease is witnessed, the Charter Stone crosses **1,000 → Tier II (Chartered House)**. Ledger-sight grows: in the Corn Hall he suddenly sees every witnessed contract in the room — and sees Dain's factors' contracts. Demonstrate it in action (he spots which merchant is lying about capacity).
7. Close: Crell, at the quay, discovers no barges are available to hire. Lys watches him understand. Then the favour lands: Veyra's courier: "Madam calls in one favour early. Korrath's Vault is going to make you an offer. Accept it." Unanswered question.

**State out:** 60 barges; Veyra 20% partner; Tier II, Standing 1,140. Barley 4 marks.

### Chapter 5 — A Debt with Teeth (Highsun 2–6)
Time-skip about four weeks (summarise quickly in the opening via texture: no rain; dust in the Harrowdales; barley at **9 marks**).
**Beats:**
1. Opening: the stay ends Emberwane 9; Dain's 41,000 note still stands. Barley at 9 marks means the barley stock is worth about 8,100 crowns — nowhere near. Barge profits won't come until the Concord must import.
2. At the Exchange (a Lesser Hammer day), Vault-Warden Sethra approaches Lys **publicly** with Korrath's offer — a public challenge: accept Korrath's credit under a vow witnessed by the whole floor, or be seen to refuse a god.
3. Negotiation at the Vault of Vows (vaulted hall of oath-rings, cold air). The first offer is a straight oath-loan with Lys's labour as collateral. Lys reframes it as **revenue-based financing / a venture stake**: **55,000 crowns** now; repay **66,000** by end of next year OR the Vault elects **15% of Rain-Tithe revenue for 10 years**. Sethra adds Korrath's price: the **famine covenant** (20,000 sacks at 4 marks to the poor-houses if the price reaches 12). Lys tries to strike it; can't. He accepts because the alternative is death in 90 days.
4. The Oathbrand is laid on his left wrist — it burns. Cost made physical.
5. Turning point/scene: Lys walks into House Dain's counting room in the upper city with Mira and Pell and pays the **41,000** in full, in Vault drafts. Aurel Dain, unruffled, returns the note. Dialogue: two worldviews. Dain: "The Seats are few because the sky is small." Lys does not burn the note theatrically; he hands it to Pell to file. (Show restraint.)
6. Remaining ~14,000 crowns: pays the small creditors (6,200), repairs Mourngate, begins warehouse expansion. Standing ~2,300.
7. Close (cost): at night Lys calculates: at 12 marks the covenant costs him (12−4)×20,000 marks = 160,000 marks = **8,000 crowns** of lost value, and the price is climbing. He's betting on famine while vowed to feed it. The brand throbs. He realises Veyra knew Korrath's price in advance.

**State out:** Free of Dain's note. Owes Korrath. Famine covenant live. Standing ~2,300.

### Chapter 6 — Raising the Banner (Emberwane 1, Dawn Auction)
**Beats:**
1. Opening: preparation the night before the Dawn Auction. Lot 12: the **district charter of Low Wharrow** — derelict warehouses, failing levees. Tier II required. The Concord needs storage for 60,000 sacks of imported grain on Harvestmoon 1 and assumes they'll take Low Wharrow cheaply.
2. Due diligence: Lys, Pell, and Garrow Finch walk the levees at night. The charter carries levee upkeep obligations — a hidden liability the Concord hasn't read. Lys decides to make sure every bidder reads it (publicly asks Oren to read the full terms aloud at the Exchange — legitimate, shocking).
3. Consortium: Garrow's Wharrow Brotherhood pledges grace in exchange for a stake and guaranteed wages (employee ownership). Veyra adds shadow-note backing. Lys is building a coalition, not a house.
4. The auction: tense, longer than Ch 2. The reading of the levee clause makes some houses drop. Dain and Vantheil keep bidding. Lys uses the price signal: he doesn't have to win at any cost — he makes the Concord pay to win or lets them fold. At the edge, Dain folds. Cinderwick wins.
5. Raising the banner: the guild's cinder-and-wick banner raised over Low Wharrow at dusk; dockers cheering; Mira's face.
6. Consequence: the Concord now must rent storage AND barges from Cinderwick to meet its forward contracts. Standing ~4,100.
7. Close: the Oathbrand flares hot during the celebration. Pell runs up with the Corn Hall slate: barley **11 marks**, bread-grain rising. One mark from the covenant trigger. Decision looming.

**State out:** Low Wharrow held. Standing ~4,100. Barley 11.

### Chapter 7 — The Cartel Break (Emberwane 6–24)
**Beats:**
1. Opening: the price hits **12 marks** at the Corn Hall. The brand burns. Covenant live: 20,000 sacks at 4 marks to the poor-houses. Lys has 18,000 sacks (minus the 1,200 stolen and later repaid in coin — so ~16,800 physical sacks) — he must buy the shortfall at 12 to sell at 4. Mira and Pell argue: default and let Korrath take his labour? No. He delivers. Cost: huge.
2. The Concord strikes: coordinated refusal to sell or ship to Cinderwick, and a rumour that Cinderwick's Vault loan is in default so Korrath will call it (fake debt scandal) — to panic his partners.
3. Game theory: the Concord is seven houses with one shared short position; each would rather settle alone than go down together (prisoner's dilemma). Lys meets **Lady Benedetta Oskel** privately: the first house to settle gets barge space and storage at a fair rate and grain at **8 marks**; later houses pay 14. He shows her the truth, not a fabrication.
4. At the Lantern Market, Lys (with Veyra) buys shadow notes that pay if Concord houses fail to deliver on Harvestmoon 1 — shorting their credit. He doesn't lie; he times true information: publishes the covenant deliveries to the poor-houses openly, which proves his loan is not in default.
5. Turning point: the famine covenant delivery — carts of grain at 4 marks rolling into the poor-houses of the lower city. People pray to Korrath and Sael over kept promises. Grace floods the Stone. Standing crosses **5,000 → Tier III (Warden House)**. New Ledger-sight: **strain** — he looks at the Concord's forward contracts and sees them glowing red-black near breaking; Dain's is the worst.
6. Oskel defects; then Merrow and Hale. Concord fractures. Dain isolated with 25,000 sacks owed.
7. Close: Aurel Dain comes to Tallow Street alone, on foot, for the first time. "You've taught every house that the Concord can be broken. Do you imagine you'll be exempt from the lesson?" Decision/unanswered question.

**State out:** Tier III, Standing ~7,900. Covenant fulfilled (major grace). Concord broken; Dain isolated.

### Chapter 8 — The Blessing License (Harvestmoon 1, Dawn Auction)
**Beats:**
1. Opening: dawn on Harvestmoon 1: House Dain fails to deliver part of its forward grain; the defaults ripple. Lys climbs the Stair with his shadow-note gains and grace.
2. Lot 3 is rare: **the Weigher's License** — Sael's blessing to operate a divinely witnessed counting-house in Vessant: to issue notes that temples accept as grace-backed. A bank license. Every great house wants it. Dain bids with borrowed grace from his remaining allies.
3. Moral centre: with a bank license Cinderwick could become a parasite on faith (skim grace off every transaction) or a patron of growth (lend to farmers, carters, dockers). Oren presses him; Mira presses him differently.
4. Insight (understanding trigger): gods don't want a lump sum; they want **recurring grace flow**. Lys stops bidding a lump sum and asks Sael, by the auction's old rules (Oren knows the clause — "a bidder may offer terms"), to accept a structured bid: a smaller up-front sum plus a fixed tithe of every note the counting-house issues, anchored to a city-wide supply network that the guild already runs (barges, Low Wharrow, Mourngate, the Rain-Tithe). He's changing the auction's game.
5. Sael's mask turns toward him. Silence. The Hammer falls for Cinderwick. The floor gasps — the gods have told everyone what they actually value.
6. Consequences: the guild goes from rich to powerful; also a target of every house. Standing ~13,600. Veyra's reaction: delighted and slightly wary.
7. Close (revelation): Oren, pale, shows Lys a notice: House Dain's 30-year emberstone concession in the **Cindermaw** comes up for renewal at the Deep Hammer on Harvestmoon 15 — and Maruth's lots are sold only to those who have "stood in the dark." Dain will pledge everything he has left.

**State out:** Weigher's License held. Standing ~13,600.

### Chapter 9 — The Sovereign Ledger (Harvestmoon 14–15)
**Beats:**
1. Opening: descent. Maruth's rule: every bidder must personally walk the Deepway to the first gate of the Cindermaw and return. Lys and Mira go down with lanterns (emberstone lamps). Real danger: heat, collapse, dungeon things (keep them strange and brief — something that feeds on light). Ledger-sight reveals the dungeon itself is a contract — Maruth's oath with the mountain — and shows the red-black strain of Dain's concession: Dain has been under-paying Maruth's tithe for years via a cooked tally.
2. Aurel Dain also walks the Deepway; they meet in the dark. Honest conversation between enemies. Dain is not a monster; he believes order requires few hands on the levers.
3. The Deep Hammer at the Cindermaw mouth: Maruth's masked figure in the heat. Dain pledges his house's remaining grace and his Seat as collateral. Lys builds a combined bid: grace, plus Rain-Tithe revenue, barge network, bank-license float, and a commitment to fair miners' shares (Garrow's dockers' model). He also — deliberately — does **not** expose Dain's cooked tally publicly; instead he brings it to Maruth's mask privately via the bidding terms ("I will pay the tithe the Cindermaw is actually owed"). Maruth understands.
4. Win. Dain is ruined; his Seat is forfeit to his creditors. Standing ~19,200 — just under Tier IV.
5. Close: Lys stands in the dark mouth of the mine that now answers to him and realises the power is complete enough to become exactly what the Concord was. Veyra, waiting outside: "Congratulations, Master Vey. You're the cartel now." Emotional/decision close.

**State out:** Cindermaw concession held. Dain ruined. Standing ~19,200.

### Chapter 10 — A Realm in Brass (Mistmoon 2–5)
**Beats:**
1. Opening: Mistmoon 2, the Harrowdales. Lys stands in a dry field with Mira and village elders who pledged in Chapter 2. The first blessed rain falls — Ilvenne honouring the Rain-Tithe. The pledges are kept. A flood of grace. The Charter Stone (via Oren's temple tally-glass or the Stone on return) crosses **20,000 → Tier IV (Sovereign House)**.
2. Ledger-sight at Tier IV: for a heartbeat he sees the whole city's web of obligations — and nearly collapses. Consequence: he now can bid for **sovereign charters** (land with lordship).
3. Aurel Dain, ruined, comes to him. Lys could buy Dain's debts and destroy the house. Instead he offers a structured settlement and a position — Dain's competence in exchange for Dain's name on an honest charter. Dain's answer is uncertain but not refusal.
4. Veyra calls in her true favour: she wants a Seat — Dain's forfeited Seat — and Lys's support at the Guild Court. The Syndicate wants to come out of the dark. Lys agrees on conditions.
5. The choice: Sael's notice — a Sovereign Charter of the Harrowdales (lordship of the valleys) will be offered at the next Dawn Auction. Lys, in the guildhall with Mira, Pell, Oren, Hobb, Garrow: he drafts the Company's charter — shares to members, dockers, farmers; limits on any single hand; the Stone as public ledger. Not a lord; a Company with a realm. Mira: "A realm in brass, then." (tie to the title).
6. Close: the Stone's final notification (Tier IV, Sovereign House). Then an unanswered question: Oren brings a sealed invitation in a script none of them can read — from above the Exchange, the **High Hammer**, where kingdoms are sold. Lys's hand throbs where the Oathbrand still lives. He says yes.

**State out:** Tier IV, Standing ~26,400. Book One closes; hook for Book Two.

## 7. Consistency rules for writers
- Lys is **he**. Mira is **she**. Veyra is **she**. Oren is **he**. Pell is **he**.
- Never reveal Theo's secret to any character.
- Gods never unmask and never speak at length. Sael speaks at most a few words per chapter.
- The Oathbrand exists only from Chapter 5 on.
- Ledger-sight limits by tier are hard rules (see §2).
- Do not kill or permanently maim a named character in Book One.
- Don't recap previous chapters heavily; one or two lines of orientation at most.
